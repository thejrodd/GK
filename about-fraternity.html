Since 1911

The History of Kappa Alpha Psi

Over a century of achievement, brotherhood, and service — from a small campus in Indiana to a global fraternity.

1911

The Founding

On January 5, 1911, Elder Watson Diggs and nine other founders established Kappa Alpha Nu (later Kappa Alpha Psi) at Indiana University, Bloomington, Indiana. The fraternity was born from a vision of achievement for Black men in higher education.

1915

A Name for the Ages

The fraternity's name was formally changed from Kappa Alpha Nu to Kappa Alpha Psi, adopting the Greek letters that would come to represent excellence and brotherhood across the nation.

1920s

National Expansion

Kappa Alpha Psi expanded rapidly throughout historically Black colleges and universities (HBCUs), establishing chapters from coast to coast. The fraternity became a symbol of academic excellence and civic engagement.

1940s

Service in Wartime

Members of Kappa Alpha Psi served with distinction in World War II. Despite the challenges of a segregated military, Kappa men demonstrated valor and leadership. The fraternity grew internationally during this decade.

1974

Incorporated Under Indiana Law

Kappa Alpha Psi Fraternity was formally incorporated under the laws of the State of Indiana, solidifying its organizational structure and legal standing as it continued to grow nationally.

2000s

Global Reach

With over 700 chapters established across the United States and internationally, Kappa Alpha Psi entered the new millennium as one of the most influential Black Greek-letter organizations in the world.

Present

A Living Legacy

Today, Kappa Alpha Psi counts over 160,000 initiated members worldwide. The fraternity's Guide Right program continues to mentor young men, and chapters like Gamma Kappa carry the torch into the next generation.

What We Stand For

Core Values

Achievement

We pursue excellence in every field of human endeavor — academic, professional, civic, and personal.

Brotherhood

The bond between Kappa men is unbreakable — forged through shared sacrifice, trust, and lifelong commitment.

Service

We serve our communities with intention — mentoring youth, supporting civic causes, and investing in the future.
